Copyright & Intellectual Property

Applies to: All employees, contractors, and interns Owner: Operations / Legal Questions: legal@plei.com Last reviewed: Q1 2026

This document establishes Plei's standards for using, sharing, and creating content that may be subject to copyright. Violations expose the company to legal liability and can result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.

1. What copyright covers

Copyright applies automatically to original works the moment they are created, including written content, images and graphic design, photographs, music and audio, video, software code, logos and brand assets, social posts, data compilations, and presentations. Assume everything is copyrighted unless you can verify otherwise.

2. What is not protected

Generally not copyrightable (though other protections may apply): raw facts and data, titles, names, slogans and short phrases, ideas/concepts/methods (the expression is protected, not the idea itself), public-domain works, and U.S. government publications.

When in doubt, treat it as protected. Do not rely on assumptions.

3.2 Written content & text

  • Do not copy and paste text from websites, articles, or publications, even partially.
  • Paraphrase and cite the source when referencing third-party content.
  • If you must quote, keep it brief (1–2 sentences) and attribute clearly.
  • Using AI to rewrite or spin copyrighted text is not a safe workaround.

3.3 Music & audio

  • Do not use commercial music in Plei videos, reels, ads, or presentations without a sync license.
  • Do not publish content with copyrighted music playing in the background.
  • Use royalty-free or licensed music only. Approved: Epidemic Sound, Artlist, YouTube Audio Library.

3.4 Social media content

  • Do not repost another brand's or individual's content without explicit written permission.
  • Native share features (IG Repost, X Retweet) are generally acceptable but do not transfer rights.
  • Crediting the creator does not substitute for permission.

3.5 Software & code

  • Do not copy code from paid platforms, proprietary repos, or non-permissive open-source licenses.
  • Check the license type (MIT, Apache, GPL, etc.) and ensure compliance.
  • Do not import third-party code into Plei's codebase without engineering review.

4. Fair use, what it is and isn't

Fair use is a legal defense, not blanket permission. Courts weigh purpose (commercial vs educational), nature (factual vs creative), amount used, and market impact. Fair use does not cover using a competitor's copy "for inspiration," reproducing an image because it's cropped, using licensed music because it's "just a few seconds," or sharing a paywalled article in full internally. If you're asking whether something counts as fair use, escalate before using.

5. Creating original work at Plei

Work made for hire: any original work created by employees in the scope of their employment belongs to Plei, Inc., not the individual. This covers marketing copy and assets, software and internal systems, content and posts, and documentation. Contractors must have IP assignment confirmed in their agreement; if a contract lacks this clause, flag it to Operations.

AI-generated content can be used, but review each platform's commercial-use terms, use judgment on commercial applications, and never input proprietary Plei data, confidential contracts, or personal employee/user information into public AI tools without authorization.

6. Brand & third-party logos

  • Do not use partner, sponsor, or third-party logos without express written approval.
  • Do not alter, stretch, recolor, or modify third-party logos.
  • Approved partner logos and usage guidelines live in the Plei Brand Assets folder.

7. When you're not sure

Stop. Don't publish or use the content until you have clarity. Escalate to legal@plei.com. Do not make unilateral fair-use judgments on behalf of the company.

8. Responding to infringement claims

If Plei receives a copyright claim, DMCA notice, or cease-and-desist: do not respond directly, forward immediately to legal; preserve records and do not delete or alter the flagged content until legal reviews; document the source and who authorized use; and do not admit liability in any communication.

9. Consequences of violation

Infringement, even unintentional, can bring statutory damages up to $150K per work (willful), legal fees and settlements, reputational damage, and platform bans. Internally, violations may result in a written warning, required retraining, or termination depending on severity and intent.
