MAGIC Hiring Process

Model: Three pass/fail barriers Team: Magic

Stage 0 — Working Backwards from the Role

Leader/Participant: Head of Magic

Before opening any Magic role:

  • Write a Job Description.
  • Define a Role FAQ: scope of work based on the role available, compensation, contract type, and work schedule.
  • Align the interview process to evaluate real-world Magic scenarios the candidate will face.
  • The hiring manager defines interviewers once the role is approved, by both the HR Manager and the Director of Operations.

Barrier 1 — The Fit Screen (Culture & Ownership)

Leader/Participant: HR Manager

Purpose: confirm alignment with Plei's mission, values, and expectations for working in a fast-paced, customer-facing environment. Duration: 30–40 minutes (video call).

Focus areas: strong mission alignment; clear communication; ownership mindset; comfort in a customer-facing environment; clear written and verbal communication in English (Spanish is a strong plus).

Decision: Pass / Fail

Facilitator Guide for Barrier 1 – Fit Screen (Culture, Communication & Ownership) Full guide

1. Purpose

Quickly assess cultural alignment, ownership mindset, communication clarity, and motivation to work in a fast-paced, customer-facing environment.

This stage is designed to filter early. Only candidates who clearly align with Plei’s mission, demonstrate accountability, and communicate effectively should move forward.

This is the candidate’s first deep impression of Plei and Magic, so the experience should feel structured, warm, and human.

2. Format

Duration: 30–40 minutes (video call)
Facilitator: Magic roles → HR Manager
Style: Conversational, structured, and friendly. Balance warmth with clarity — this is not a technical interview, but standards are high.

3. Focus Areas & Example Questions

3.1 Mission Resonance – “Why Plei & Why Magic?”
Focus Area: Does the candidate genuinely connect with Plei’s mission and the Magic role, beyond the need for a job?

  • What excites you about Plei and this role specifically?
  • What does “great customer experience” mean to you?
  • How do you think sports — or soccer in particular — can bring people together?
What to look for: Authentic interest in the mission, curiosity about the role and impact, values alignment, not rehearsed answer.

3.2 Ownership & Accountability
Focus Area: Does the candidate take responsibility for their work and outcomes?

  • Tell me about a time you took ownership of a problem from start to resolution.
  • Have you ever fixed or escalated an issue that technically wasn’t your responsibility?
  • Tell me about a mistake you made at work. What did you do after?
What to look for: Ownership language (“I” vs “they”), accountability without defensiveness, willingness to learn and improve.

3.3 Communication & Clarity
Focus Area: Can the candidate communicate clearly, calmly, and professionally?

  • Tell me about a time you had to explain something difficult to a customer or teammate.
  • How do you communicate when someone is frustrated or upset?
  • How do you make sure you’re being understood?
What to look for: Clear, thoughtful communication, emotional awareness, ability to listen and respond, not react. Clear written and verbal communication in English (Spanish is a strong plus).

3.4 Comfort in a Real-Time, Customer-Facing Environment
Focus Area: Is the candidate comfortable operating in a live, fast-moving environment?

  • How do you handle working in situations where things change quickly?
  • Tell me about a time you had to think on your feet.
  • What helps you stay calm under pressure?
What to look for: Emotional regulation, adaptability, comfort with ambiguity and pace.

4. Evaluation Framework

Pass / Fail decision — no “maybe”

Scoring Dimensions (1–5):

  • Strong mission alignment
  • Clear communication
  • Ownership mindset
  • Comfort in a customer-facing environment
  • Clear written and verbal communication in English (Spanish is a strong plus)
The candidate must score at least 3/5 in all dimensions to advance. Any 1/5 is an automatic fail.

5. Notes for Interviewer

  • Write specific, concrete feedback immediately after the call.
  • Avoid vague comments (“nice”, “good energy”).
  • Focus on examples and behaviors.
  • Remember: this stage exists to raise the bar, not to move fast.

Barrier 2 — Technical / Execution Capability

Leader/Participant: Corresponding Regional Operations Manager

Purpose: validate the candidate can independently handle the real workload, tools, and decision-making required for the role. Duration: 45–60 minutes (video call). Format: practical, scenario-based, role-specific.

Evaluation focus: role clarity & scope discipline; prioritization & workload management; problem-solving logic; communication & escalation judgment.

Decision: Pass / Fail

Facilitator Guide for Barrier 2 – Functional Fundamentals (Magic Skills) Full guide

1. Purpose

Assess whether the candidate has the practical skills, judgment, and workload capacity to perform the role effectively in a real, fast-moving Magic environment.

This stage focuses on execution ability, not culture fit. Candidates should demonstrate that they can manage volume, prioritize effectively, operate within defined scope, and solve problems using sound logic and clear escalation.

Only candidates who demonstrate strong execution readiness should advance.

2. Format

Duration: 45–60 minutes (video call)
Facilitator: Corresponding Regional Operations Manager
Style: Practical, scenario-based, role-specific

3. Focus Areas & Scenarios

3.1 Role Understanding & Scope Discipline
Focus Area: Does the candidate clearly understand what this role does and does not own?

  • You’re managing multiple games, messages, and issues at the same time. One issue escalates. What do you do first, and why?
  • How do you decide when to escalate an issue versus solving it yourself?
  • Tell me about a time you stayed within your role even though you could have taken on more.
What to look for: Clear prioritization logic, respect for role boundaries, calm, structured decision-making, no “hero” or scope-creep behavior.

3.2 Workload Management & Prioritization
Focus Area: Can the candidate manage high volume, competing priorities, and time pressure?

  • Walk me through how you manage your workload when everything feels urgent.
  • How do you organize your day when you’re responsible for multiple regions, facilities, or schedules?
  • Tell me about a time you dropped or deprioritized something intentionally. How did you decide?
What to look for: Evidence of systems or structure, thoughtful trade-offs, comfort saying “not now” when appropriate, no panic-driven or reactive behavior.

3.3 Practical Problem Solving & Execution
Focus Area: Can the candidate solve real-world Magic-style problems logically and efficiently?

  • A facility cancels last minute and players are already en route. What are your immediate steps?
  • Fill velocity is dropping in one region. What do you look at first?
  • A host is upset and blaming Magic for low attendance. How do you handle it?
What to look for: Step-by-step thinking, clear sequencing of actions, awareness of stakeholders (players, facilities, internal team), appropriate escalation points.

3.4 Communication & Escalation Judgment
Focus Area: Does the candidate know when and how to escalate issues appropriately?

  • Tell me about a time you escalated something early. What was the outcome?
  • Tell me about a time you should have escalated sooner. What did you learn?
  • How do you communicate issues upward without creating noise?
What We’re Looking For: Early risk awareness, clear, concise communication, comfort escalating without defensiveness, no hiding or over-escalating issues.

4. Evaluation Framework

Pass / Fail decision — no “maybe”

Scoring Dimensions (1–5):

  • Role clarity & scope discipline
  • Prioritization & workload management
  • Problem-solving logic
  • Communication & escalation judgment
The candidate must score at least 3/5 in all dimensions to advance. Any 1/5 is an automatic fail.

5. Notes for Interviewer

  • Focus on how they think, not the “perfect” answer.
  • Look for responsibility and calm under pressure.
  • Red flags: defensiveness, rigidity, lack of empathy.

Barrier 3 — Ownership, Judgment & Scale

Leader/Participant: Head of Magic or Operations Manager

Purpose: assess whether the candidate can be trusted with responsibility, sound judgment, and operating effectively at scale. Duration: 45–60 minutes (video call). Format: direct, reflective, and judgment-focused.

Evaluation focus: ownership & accountability; judgment under uncertainty; escalation & boundary awareness; ability to operate at scale.

Decision: Pass / Fail

Facilitator Guide for Barrier 3 – Problem-Solving & Adaptability (Live Scenarios) Full guide

1. Purpose

Evaluate whether the candidate demonstrates the judgment, accountability, and decision-making maturity required to operate responsibly within the Magic organization.

This stage focuses on how the candidate thinks, not what they know how to do. The goal is to assess whether they can own outcomes, navigate ambiguity, and make sound decisions without constant oversight.

Only candidates who demonstrate strong ownership, good judgment, and readiness to operate at scale should advance.

2. Format

Duration: 45–60 minutes (video call)
Facilitator: Head of Magic Operations or Operations Manager
Style: Direct, reflective, and judgment-focused

3. Focus Areas

3.1 Ownership & Accountability
Focus Area: Does the candidate take responsibility for outcomes — especially when things go wrong?

  • Tell me about a time something failed under your watch. What did you own?
  • When something goes wrong, how do you decide what’s your responsibility versus what to escalate?
  • Tell me about a mistake you made that had impact. What did you do next?
What We’re Looking For: Ownership language (“I,” not “they”), accountability without defensiveness, reflection and learning, no blame-shifting.

3.2 Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
Focus Area: Can the candidate make sound decisions without perfect information?

  • Tell me about a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.
  • How do you balance speed versus accuracy when the stakes are high?
  • When do you decide to act, and when do you pause?
What We’re Looking For: Reasoned decision-making, risk awareness, comfort with ambiguity, thoughtful trade-offs.

3.3 Ownership Language
Do they say “I’ll take care of this” vs “Someone should…”? Do they close the loop?

3.4 Judgment, Boundaries & Escalation
Focus Area: Does the candidate understand authority, boundaries, and when to pull others in?

  • Tell me about a time you escalated something earlier than expected. Why?
  • Tell me about a time you waited too long to escalate. What did you learn?
  • How do you decide who needs to be informed versus involved?
What We’re Looking For: Strong escalation judgment, respect for structure and roles, clear communication upward, no bypassing or overreaching.

3.5 Operating at Scale & Through Change
Focus Area: Can the candidate operate in a growing, evolving environment without breaking systems?

  • How do you adapt when processes change or are still being built?
  • Tell me about a time you had to unlearn a way of working.
  • What helps you stay effective as complexity increases?
What We’re Looking For: Flexibility without chaos, comfort with evolving systems, process-minded thinking, long-term orientation.

4. Evaluation Framework

Pass / Fail decision — no “maybe”

Scoring Dimensions (1–5):

  • Ownership & accountability
  • Judgment under uncertainty
  • Escalation & boundary awareness
  • Ability to operate at scale
The candidate must score at least 3/5 in all dimensions to advance. Any 1/5 is an automatic fail.

5. Notes for Interviewer

  • Focus on how they think, not the “perfect” answer.
  • Look for responsibility and calm under pressure.
  • Red flags: defensiveness, rigidity, lack of empathy.

Final Decision & Debrief

Leader/Participant: Head of Magic and Director of Operations

  • Debrief within 24 hours of the last interview.
  • Each interviewer submits feedback before discussion, no groupthink.
  • The hiring manager makes the decision with the bar raiser's input.
  • If pass, offer goes out within 48 hours.
