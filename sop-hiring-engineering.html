Engineering Hiring Process

Model: Five pass/fail barriers Inspired by: Amazon's bar-raiser model
The idea: every candidate must pass five critical "barriers" before an offer is made. These barriers ensure long-term success, culture fit, and high performance. Each stage has a clear pass/fail decision — no "maybe" hires.

Stage 0 — Working Backwards from the Role

Leader/Participant: Brayan

Before opening any role:

  • Write a Job Description & Career Path for the candidate after joining, describing their impact, accomplishments, and growth.
  • Define a Role FAQ: internal Q&A for expectations, scope, and success metrics.
  • Align the interview loop to measure the abilities that map directly to this future vision.
  • Depending on the role, the hiring manager leads the selection of interviewers once the role has been approved.

Barrier 1 — The Fit Screen (Culture & Ownership)

Leader/Participant: Elizabeth for leaders, Priscila for non-leaders

Purpose: quickly assess cultural alignment, communication, and ownership mindset. Format: 30–40 min video call.

Focus areas:

  • Why Plei? Mission resonance & understanding of "Building Connection Through the Beautiful Game."
  • Ownership mindset: examples of taking end-to-end responsibility.
  • Growth mindset: how they learn, adapt, and take feedback.
  • Clear communication in English (Spanish is a plus).

Decision: Pass/Fail — if they don't align with our purpose and principles, they don't move forward.

Barrier 2 — Technical / Functional Fundamentals

Leader/Participant: Brayan or team leader (Rigo / Adrian / David)

Purpose: ensure they have the core skills to perform the role without relying on others to cover for them. Format: role-specific exercise or live problem-solving session.

  • Engineering: system design + hands-on coding (AI copilot allowed, but evaluated for usage judgment).
  • Product: case study + product strategy prioritization.
  • Design: portfolio review + live design challenge.

Evaluation focus: problem decomposition, data-driven decisions, clear trade-off reasoning, and code/design/plan quality — not perfection.

Decision: must demonstrate independence in core skills.

Barrier 3 — Bar Raiser Interview

Leader/Participant: Brayan or team leader

Purpose: an independent interviewer, not on the hiring team, evaluates long-term performance potential and principles alignment. Format: 60 min deep-dive behavioral interview.

Competencies assessed: bias for action; customer obsession (players, organizers, facilities); invent & simplify; insist on the highest standards; learn & be curious; deliver results.

Decision: the bar raiser can veto the hire if they believe the candidate won't raise the team's average performance over the long term.

Barrier 4 — Problem-Solving & Adaptability

Leader/Participant: Stefano

Purpose: see how they adapt in ambiguous, changing, and high-pressure environments. Format: simulation exercise or role play.

  • Engineers: debugging incident + priority shift mid-task.
  • PMs: product change due to a market shift.

Evaluation focus: composure, prioritization, collaboration under pressure, creativity within constraints.

Decision: must handle ambiguity with structure and calm.

Barrier 5 — Leadership Principles in Action

Leader/Participant: Sebastian Duque for leaders

Purpose: final loop to confirm they model Plei's leadership behaviors and can grow beyond the role. Format: 2–3 short panel interviews with future peers and cross-functional stakeholders.

Focus areas: strategic thinking and connecting day-to-day work to long-term vision; coaching and elevating others, even if not a manager; challenging the status quo with data and reasoning; clear articulation of trade-offs to business and technical audiences.

Decision: consensus required. Any strong "no" from a panel member is a block.

Final Decision & Debrief

Leader/Participant: Brayan with Elizabeth & Sebastian D. (Priscila is updated for HR onboarding.)

  • Debrief within 24 hours of the last interview.
  • Each interviewer submits feedback before discussion, no groupthink.
  • The hiring manager makes the decision with the bar raiser's input.
  • If pass, offer goes out within 48 hours.
